# The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act 2023) - Key Provisions & Knowledge Base

## Overview
The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023) was enacted by the Parliament of India to govern the processing of digital personal data while recognising both the right of individuals to protect their personal data and the need to process such personal data for lawful purposes.

## Key Timeline & Enactment Dates
- DPDP Rules notified: 13 November 2025 (provisional implementation schedule).
- Consent Manager registration & framework: 13 November 2026.
- Full obligations and enforcement: 13 May 2027.
*(Note: Dates to be confirmed against the Rules, 2025 before go-live).*

## Core Sections and Provisions

### Section 3: Application of the Act
- Section 3(a): Applies to processing of digital personal data within the territory of India where collected in digital form or digitised subsequently.
- Section 3(b): Applies to processing outside India if connected to offering goods or services to Data Principals in India.
- Section 3(c)(ii)(A): The Act does NOT apply to personal data that is made publicly available by the Data Principal to whom such personal data relates. (Lawful basis: `made_public_by_person`).

### Section 4: Grounds for Processing Personal Data
- A person may process personal data only in accordance with the provisions of this Act and for a lawful purpose for which the Data Principal has given or is deemed to have given her consent, or for certain legitimate uses under Section 7.

### Section 5: Notice
- Prior to or at the time of requesting consent, the Data Fiduciary must give the Data Principal an itemised notice in clear and plain language.
- The notice must specify:
  1. The personal data to be processed and the specific lawful purpose.
  2. The manner in which the Data Principal may exercise rights under Section 11.
  3. The manner in which the Data Principal may make a complaint to the Data Protection Board of India.
- Must provide the option to access the notice in English or any of the 22 languages specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution.

### Section 6: Consent
- Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action.
- The Data Principal has the right to withdraw consent at any time with the ease of giving it.
- Consent may also be managed or withdrawn through a registered Consent Manager. (Lawful basis: `consented`).

### Section 7: Certain Legitimate Uses
- Section 7(a): A Data Fiduciary may process personal data for the specified purpose for which the Data Principal has voluntarily provided her personal data to the Data Fiduciary, and has not indicated that she does not consent to the use of such personal data. (Lawful basis: `gave_voluntarily`).
- *Important Compliance Note*: The Act does NOT contain a general "legitimate interest" ground for direct B2B marketing or cold outreach. Lawful processing must be grounded in Section 3(c)(ii)(A), Section 6, or Section 7(a).

### Section 8: General Obligations of Data Fiduciary
- Section 8(1): The Data Fiduciary is responsible for compliance with the Act, including processing undertaken by a Data Processor on its behalf.
- Section 8(5): Mandatory requirement to protect personal data in its possession or under its control by taking reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach.
- Section 8(6): In the event of a personal data breach, the Data Fiduciary MUST intimate the Data Protection Board of India and each affected Data Principal in the prescribed form and manner.
- Section 8(7): Data Minimisation & Purpose Limitation - A Data Fiduciary must erase personal data upon the Data Principal withdrawing consent or as soon as it is reasonable to assume that the specified purpose is no longer being served.
- Section 8(8): Must establish effective grievance redressal mechanisms.

### Section 9: Processing of Personal Data of Children and Persons with Disability
- Verifiable parental consent required before processing data of children (below 18 years).
- Prohibition on tracking, behavioral monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children.

### Section 10: Additional Obligations of Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF)
- Designation of a Data Protection Officer (DPO) based in India who is an individual reporting directly to the Board of Directors or governing body.
- Appointment of an independent data auditor to carry out periodic data audits.
- Conducting periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA) and other measures including periodic audits.

### Section 11: Rights of the Data Principal
- Section 11(1): Right to obtain summary of personal data being processed, processing activities, and identities of other Data Fiduciaries/Processors with whom data has been shared.
- Section 11(2): Right to correction, completion, updating, and erasure of personal data.
- Section 11(3): Right to grievance redressal.
- Section 11(4): Right to nominate another individual in the event of death or incapacity.

### Section 16: Cross-Border Transfer of Personal Data
- Personal data may be transferred outside India unless restricted or blacklisted by the Central Government by notification.

### Section 33 & The Schedule: Penalties
- In accordance with Section 33 and the Schedule to the Act, penalties can reach up to ₹250 crore depending on the breach.
- Specific statutory caps:
  - Failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach under Section 8(5): Penalties can reach up to ₹250 crore depending on the breach.
  - Failure to notify the Board or affected Data Principals of a personal data breach under Section 8(6): Penalties can reach up to ₹200 crore depending on the breach.
  - Non-compliance with provisions relating to children under Section 9: Penalties can reach up to ₹200 crore depending on the breach.
  - Non-compliance with significant data fiduciary obligations under Section 10: Penalties can reach up to ₹150 crore depending on the breach.
- *Strict Editorial Rule*: Never state "₹250 crore per violation". The legally accurate formulation is: "penalties can reach up to ₹250 crore depending on the breach".
